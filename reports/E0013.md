## Summary

- **Direct SFT on C3 beats C1-SFT → GRPO by a wide margin.** On the test set:
  - a scores 0.869 ± 0.038 and b 0.850 ± 0.022; E0012 A scores 0.731 ± 0.021.
  - All six a and b models beat A's best seed (0.750).
  - The pooled Wilson intervals do not overlap: a is [0.831, 0.900], A is [0.683, 0.773].
  - **H15: "SFT directly on C3 is better than GRPO from C1-SFT"** (+0.138, threshold +0.05).
- **GRPO on top of C3-SFT adds nothing.**
  - c-s17 scores 0.883 on the test set: +0.014 over a's mean, −0.008 against its own start a-s17 (0.892).
  - Its validation win rate stayed between 0.9125 and 0.9375 for all 60 batches.
  - The training batches were won 96.5 % of the time. Of a batch's 4 groups only 0.73 on average had both wins and
    losses, and 28 of the 60 batches had none, so there was little signal to learn from.
  - **H16 does not hold.** c has only one seed.
- **The C1-SFT start (r) scores 0.333 ± 0.017 on the test set.** For r-s17, which is E0012's start checkpoint, the test
  score is 0.317; its validation score in E0012 was 0.275.
- **Nothing generalises to C2 or C4.**
  - Every group wins 0 or almost 0 out of domain.
  - b, the group that saw two levels, wins 3 of 240 episodes on C2 and 1 of 240 on C4; a wins 0 of 240 on both.
  - **H17: b is numerically above a on both levels, but the difference is 4 episodes out of 960.**
  - Most episodes end with a `cook` command; in tw-cooking, a cooking mistake loses the game. On C3, which has one
    ingredient, the SFT models learned the cooking steps; on C2 and C4, which have 2–3 ingredients, they get the
    cooking wrong.
- **c's p_t is poorly calibrated and saturated** (H18: 1 of 3 checks passes).
  - Offset: 0.142, not below 0.1.
  - Brier: 0.189, against the constant baseline of 0.186, so not below it.
  - Step-adjusted AUC gain: 0.133, above 0.05.
  - Out of domain the mean p is 0.984 on C2 and 0.946 on C4, against E0012 A's 0.777 and 0.587.
  - 45 % of the test predictions are within 0.005 of 0 or 1; A's figure is 5 %.

## Executor notes

### Different from the issue

- **c ran for seed 17 only.** c-s23 and c-s29 were skipped by the issue's rule (selected a checkpoint at 0.95 ≥ 0.95).
  The c row is therefore one seed, not a mean of three.
- The E0012 A comparison row comes from `reports/E0012.md`. All its seeds started from E0010b-s17, while r uses each
  seed's own C1-SFT checkpoint. A's three seeds differ only in the GRPO training seed.
- No other deviations. Hyperparameters, data, H and the evaluation protocol are as in the issue. No code in `jevtw/`
  was changed for E0013 (T0010 is in PR #40).

### Problems

- None that stopped a job. Every job ran once and completed.
- a-s29 landed on kn116, which was slow (about 1.8 times as long per epoch); kn116 is not on the exclusion list.
- A side observation, not a problem: the SFT models' own logit readout was never trained, so it is saturated. Their
  validation mean p is 0.999 with a mean logit of 9–12. c starts from that, and its Platt scaling (α = 0.1, 60 batches)
  only partly undoes it.

### Hypotheses (C3 test, mean over seeds)

- **H15 (Q1):** a 0.869 − E0012 A 0.731 = **+0.138 > +0.05**. Conclusion: **direct imitation learning on C3 is
  better than GRPO from C1-SFT.**
  - All three a seeds (0.892 / 0.892 / 0.825) are above A's best seed (0.750), and the pooled Wilson intervals do
    not overlap.
  - b (0.850) leads to the same conclusion.
- **H16 (Q2):** c 0.883 (one seed) − a 0.869 = **+0.014 < +0.05**. Against its own start a-s17 (0.892) it is
  −0.008. **H16 does not hold**: GRPO did not improve on in-domain SFT.
  - Validation stayed flat at 0.91–0.94 for 60 batches.
  - For the two seeds where a reached 0.95 on validation, c was not run at all.
- **H17 (Q3, generalisation):**
  - Every group wins almost nothing on C2 or C4:
    - r, a, c and E0012 A: 0 wins on both levels
    - b: 3 / 240 on C2 (0.013) and 1 / 240 on C4 (0.004)
  - b is numerically above a on both levels, but 4 wins in 480 episodes is within noise: a's 0 / 240 has a Wilson upper
    bound of 0.016.
  - Seeing C1 as well as C3 does not teach the 2–3-ingredient recipes. Most losses end with a cooking mistake.
- **H18 (calibration, c-s17):**
  - Offset **0.142 (not < 0.1)**.
  - Brier **0.189, not below the constant baseline 0.186**.
  - Step-adjusted AUC gain **0.133 (> 0.05, holds)**.
  - Out of domain the mean p is **0.984 on C2 and 0.946 on C4, higher (worse) than E0012 A's 0.777 and 0.587**.
  - 45 % of test predictions are saturated, against 5 % for A.
  - **1 of 3 in-domain checks holds**, and out of domain c is more over-confident than A. The ranking information
    (AUC 0.807, within-game AUC 0.753) is there. The level is not: the SFT start has a saturated readout, and 60
    batches of Platt scaling do not fix it.

### Interpretation

- On C3 the walkthrough is a near-perfect teacher. Five epochs of SFT on 200 walkthroughs reach 0.87 on the test set.
  GRPO from C1-SFT, with 60 batches of 4 games, reaches 0.73.
- Once the policy wins about 95 % of its training games, GRPO with G = 8 has almost no non-zero advantages left, and
  c confirms there is nothing more for it to gain.
- The interesting gap is now out of domain: no method gets beyond about 1 % on C2 or C4.
