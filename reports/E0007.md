# E0007: CoT diagnosis on fixed states — what the model reasons under `full` vs `feedback` history

Issue #24. Diagnosis only: no training, no change in `jevtw/`. Base model `Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B` (bf16), one Slurm job
(7999114, L40S, 1 h 01 min, completed, exit 0, commit `d8c37e2`), output `runs/E0007/cot.jsonl` (3200 rows, not in git).

## Setup

- **States**: the walkthrough states of E0006 (`scripts/diagnose_history.walk`) whose walkthrough action is
  `prepare meal` or `eat meal`: L0 40 + 40, L1 40 + 40 = 160 states (`data/games_L0/valid`, `data/games_L1/valid`).
- **Settings** (history window 8, `forecast=False`):

  | name | history | format | max reasoning tokens |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `full_plain` | full (V1) | plain | 320 |
  | `full_chat` | full (V1) | chat_nothink | 128 |
  | `feedback_plain` | feedback (V2) | plain | 320 |
  | `feedback_chat` | feedback (V2) | chat_nothink | 128 |

- **Sampling**: 5 reasonings per state and setting (160 × 4 × 5 = 3200), each with its own generator seeded from
  sha256(game, step, history, format, k). `LMPolicy.sample_reasoning_batch` samples the reasoning,
  `LMPolicy.decision_forward` (no grad) gives q over the options, and the action is drawn from q with the same
  generator. These are the three steps of `LMPolicy.act`; the CPU test checks that a row equals `LMPolicy.act` with
  the same seed.
- **Re-process mass**: sum of q over the options whose command starts with `cook`, `chop`, `slice`, `dice`, `fry`,
  `roast`, `grill`. On L0 the only such options are `cook … with oven / stove` (L0 has no cut commands).
- **Keyword tags** (regular expressions in `scripts/diagnose_cot.py`): `notices_done` ("already fried", "is diced",
  "are chopped", …) and `plans_process` ("need to / should / must / next … fry / roast / cook / chop / slice / dice",
  within one sentence). They do not see negation.
- **Code**: `scripts/diagnose_cot.py`, `scripts/slurm/diagnose_cot.sbatch`, `scripts/analyze_cot.py`,
  `tests/test_e0007_cot.py` (4 CPU tests). Numbers: `reports/E0007.json`; reading labels: `reports/E0007_labels.json`.

Things to keep in mind when reading the numbers:

- The 5 samples of a state are not independent states. Each cell below has 200 reasonings from 40 states.
- The E0006 rows (no reasoning) come from `runs/E0006/teacher_forced.jsonl`, which stores only the five most
  likely options, so their re-process mass is a lower bound ("≥"). The CoT rows use the whole distribution.
- "sampled = a\*" is one draw per reasoning from q; "a\* rank 1" is the argmax. They agree closely because q after a
  finished reasoning is almost one-hot.

- **`prepare`, L1**: all four reasoning settings are between 0.17 and 0.29; full without reasoning is 0.298,

## 3. Human reading (L0 `prepare`, 20 reasonings per setting)

Sample: `random.Random("7|<setting>").sample(...)` over the 200 L0 `prepare` reasonings of each setting
(`scripts/analyze_cot.py`, `reading_sample`). I read all 80 in full; one reader, no second pass by someone else.

How I applied the classes:

- **A**: the reasoning says that the ingredient is already processed (or reads it off the inventory line) and
  concludes `prepare meal`.
- **B**: the reasoning treats a recipe direction as still to do ("the next step is to dice / roast / fry it").
  This includes reasonings that plan to dice or slice, find no such option (L0 has none), and stop there.
- **C**: the reasoning concludes the right step and the distribution after it goes elsewhere.
- **D**: other confusion (take another ingredient, take the cookbook, drink the water, contradictory text).
- **E**: `prepare meal` is chosen and the text gives no valid reason: none at all, or a wrong one ("the oven is
  empty, so cooking is not possible", "prepare the meal by frying the red onion").

| full_plain | 5 | 14 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 6 |
| full_chat | 1 | 9 | 0 | 1 | 9 | 0 |
| feedback_plain | 3 | 14 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 5 |
| feedback_chat | 0 | 12 | 0 | 1 | 7 | 3 |
| all | 9 | 49 | 0 | 4 | 18 | 14 |

What I saw:

- **No C.** In none of the 80 does the choice go against the conclusion of the text. When the reasoning ends with
  "Answer:", q puts 0.95–1.00 on the option the text names.
- **B is the largest class in every setting, also with the full history** (14 of 20 in `full_plain`). The typical
  text reads the inventory line ("diced roasted lettuce") and still says "the next step is to roast the lettuce".
- **14 of the 49 B reasonings rank `prepare meal` first anyway.** In 13 of them the plan is to dice or slice, which
  is not an option on L0; 10 of the 14 texts are cut at the cap. The choice is right for a reason that is not in
  the text. When the planned step is roast or fry, the option exists and is taken.
- **Chat reasonings are too short to carry the argument.** 16 of the 40 are E: a correct choice after a wrong or
  empty reason. Only 1 of 40 is A.
- **A is rare under the feedback history** (3 of 40), although the current observation still contains the
  inventory line with "fried" / "roasted" / "diced" in both history modes.

states under feedback the model continues the recipe after the meal exists: a `cook …` action is sampled in
144 of 200 L0 `eat` samples for `feedback_chat` (96 of them `cook meal with stove`) and in 101 of 200 for
`feedback_plain` (67 `cook meal with stove`). The added reading of 80 `eat`
reasonings (section 3) agrees: under feedback 37 of 40 are B, 1 is A, 1 is C; under full 27 of 40 are A and 10 are
B. So at `eat`, unlike at `prepare`, the misreading does depend on the history.

**Q3. How much does noticing matter for the choice?** By my reading it decides it: the 9 class-A reasonings have
mean q(a\*) 1.00 and the 49 class-B ones 0.27 (and those 0.27 come mostly from the "dice is not an option" cases).
By the `notices_done` tag the difference is small except in `full_chat` (0.875 against 0.532), but that tag has
recall 0.44. The `plans_process` tag gives the clearer split: q(a\*) 0.12–0.32 with the tag against 0.25–0.90
without.

**Can reasoning replace the recipe copies in the history?** With the base model, no, on this evidence:

1. At `eat` the feedback history stays at q(a\*) 0.03–0.21 with reasoning, against 0.64–0.85 for full.
2. At `prepare` the two histories become equal only because reasoning costs the full history 0.22–0.33 of q(a\*)
   on L0 (0.748 → 0.420 / 0.525).
3. The reasoning that would be needed (class A) appears in 3 of 40 feedback samples and 6 of 40 full samples.
   At `eat` (added reading) it appears in 1 of 40 feedback samples and 27 of 40 full samples.

This is about the untrained model. Whether training changes how the model reasons about the recipe is not tested
here.

### Open questions for the planner / user

1. The plain format hits the 320-token cap in up to 61 % of the samples, and the capped texts are the ones that go
   in circles ("wait, the next step is to dice it…"). Is a diagnosis of plain with a larger cap wanted, or is the
   chat format the one to continue with? I have not run anything beyond the issue.
2. The reasoning policy at step 0 of training has q(a\*) 0.42–0.53 at L0 `prepare` in every setting. If the next
   experiment trains a reasoning policy, the history mode matters at `eat` (full 0.64–0.85, feedback 0.03–0.21),
   and much less at `prepare`.
