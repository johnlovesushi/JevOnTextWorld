## Summary

- The collapse comes from the prompt. On the same states, the base model on L0 ranks the walkthrough action first in
  76.9 % of the states with full history (V1) and in 45.6 % with feedback history (V2).
- The loss is in the last two steps. `prepare meal`: 0.85 -> 0.20. `eat meal`: 0.42 -> 0.00.
- In feedback mode the model cooks the ingredient again (it is already cooked), which ends the game. 73 of the 74
  lost episodes of E0005h end with a `cook` command.
- The recipe is in the current observation in both modes, so feedback mode does not hide it. What it removes is the
  full text of earlier steps. One full step (V3) restores `prepare meal` (0.70) but not `eat meal` (0.00).
- Part 3: V3 (full, window 1), the best of V3-V5, wins 0 / 80 on L0. E0005a (V1) wins 23 / 80, E0005h (V2) 0 / 80.
- H-a is partly supported (full text in the history matters, but more than one copy of the recipe is needed to
  explain V1). H-b is not supported. Details in section 6.
- Follow-up (section 5b): three changes to the feedback prompt (no duplicated feedback line; inventory after every
  past step; a sentence against repeating finished steps) were scored on the same states. None helps: base model,
  L0, top-1 0.450 / 0.431 / 0.419 against 0.456 for V2, and `eat meal` stays at 0.00.
- Second follow-up (section 5c): the room description (R), the inventory (I) and the notes with the recipe (N)
  were put back into the past steps of the feedback history in all combinations. **The recipe copies are the
  necessary part**: without N nothing helps, with N the model stops cooking again (mass on `cook` 0.59 -> 0.12).
  But no shorter history works on both levels. On L0 only R + I + N (= full) reaches `eat meal` 0.42 (R + N 0.23,
  I + N 0.07). On L1, I + N is better than full (top-1 0.578 against 0.498, 733 against 1251 tokens).

## Method

Every game is played along its walkthrough (`metadata.walkthrough` in the game's `.json`). The only command
skipped is the opening `inventory`, which `TextWorldEnv` filters out of the options. At every state on that
path the prompt is built once per variant and `LMPolicy.decision_forward` scores all options (no reasoning,
`forecast=False`). All variants see the same states, so the differences come from the prompt alone.

| variant | history_mode | history_window |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | full | 8 |
| V2 | feedback | 8 |
| V3 | full | 1 |
| V4 | feedback | 1 |
| V5 | (none) | 0 |

a\* is the walkthrough action, K the number of options, "top-1" the share of states where a\* has the highest
score, "rank" the position of a\* among the K options (1 is best).

### Interpretation

**The collapse is an effect of the prompt, not of sampling.** With the states held fixed, the base model on L0
ranks the walkthrough action first in 76.9 % of the states under V1 and in 45.6 % under V2; mean log q(a\*) falls
from -0.73 to -3.57. The loss is concentrated in the last two steps: `prepare meal` 0.85 -> 0.20 and `eat meal`
0.42 -> 0.00. The probability of sampling the whole walkthrough (product of q(a\*), mean over games) is 0.236
under V1 and 0.002 under V2, next to the measured win rates 23 / 80 = 0.29 (E0005a) and 0 / 80 (E0005h).

**What the model does instead** is cook again. Under V2 at the `prepare meal` state it puts 0.59 of its mass on
`cook <ingredient> with stove / oven` (0.16 on `prepare meal`), although the inventory line says the ingredient is
already fried / roasted; at the `eat meal` state it puts 0.74 on `cook meal with ...`. Cooking a cooked item burns
it and ends the game. The sampled episodes show the same thing (section 4): in feedback mode 74 of 80 episodes are
lost, 73 of them with a `cook` command as the last command, 47 at step 3 or 4, and `prepare meal` is chosen 4
times in total against 46 with full history. The model reads the recipe line "fry the red onion" as something
still to do. There is no sign of the loops the issue asked about: `examine cookbook` is 17 % of the commands in
both modes and three in a row happens in 2 and 4 episodes.

**H-a (the repeated recipe is the hint): partly supported, but the statement needs a correction.**
- The recipe is never missing. After `examine cookbook` the Notes section, with "Directions: ... prepare meal", is
  part of *every* current observation, in both modes (see the prompts in section 3). Feedback mode removes the
  copies in the history, not the recipe.
- Full text in the history does help: with one history step, V3 (full) against V4 (feedback) gives 0.70 against
  0.20 on `prepare meal` (base, L0).
- But copies of the recipe alone do not explain V1. At the `eat meal` state V3 shows the recipe twice and the
  inventory "a meal" twice, and still never ranks `eat meal` first (0 / 40; 0.46 of the mass goes to taking
  another ingredient). Only V1, where the history shows the whole path (cookbook, take, prepare, each with the
  full observation), reaches 0.42. So what helps is several full past observations, not one more copy of the
  recipe. Whether the useful part is the recipe text or the inventory / room text of the earlier steps cannot be
  separated with the existing configurations (both are in the full observation).
- **Section 5c separates them, and H-a comes out mostly right.** The copies of the recipe after the earlier steps
  are necessary: without them the room and the inventory do nothing (V11, V14 = V2), with them the re-cooking
  stops (mass on `cook` at the L0 `prepare` state 0.59 -> 0.12, for every combination). They are not sufficient
  on L0: recipe copies alone give `prepare meal` 0.38 and `eat meal` 0.05, and the room and inventory text add the
  rest (0.85 and 0.42 with all three). On L1 recipe copies alone already match the full history (top-1 0.521
  against 0.498, `eat meal` 0.62 against 0.62), and recipe + inventory beats it.
- This also means the base model is weak here even in the best case: 0.42 on `eat meal` under V1 is the reason
  E0005a wins only 23 of 80.

**H-b (the feedback shown twice makes the model think nothing changed): not supported.**
- V2 and V4 both have the duplicated line; they differ in how much history there is and give nearly the same
  numbers (top-1 0.456 and 0.456, mean rank 5.42 and 5.19). V5 has no duplicate (no history at all) and is the
  worst (0.350, 6.19). Removing the history is not a remedy.
- Under V2 the model does not behave as if nothing had happened: its preferred action changes with the state
  (take the ingredient, then cook it, then cook the meal).
- The strict test was added afterwards (section 5b, V6): the V2 prompt without the duplicate scores the same as
  V2 (top-1 0.450 against 0.456 on L0, 0.348 against 0.351 on L1, base model).

**The trained checkpoint (E0003c, batch 100)** was trained with the V1 layout and depends on it even more: L0 top-1
0.93 under V1, 0.61-0.68 under V2-V4; `eat meal` 0.90 under V1 and at most 0.05 elsewhere on L0. It has learned
`prepare meal` in a way that transfers to the other layouts (0.75-0.85 on L0 for V2-V4), but not `eat meal`.

**Part 3** agrees with the teacher-forced numbers: V3 has the best mean rank among V3-V5 and still wins 0 / 80,
because `eat meal` is never preferred. It does choose `prepare meal` (33 episodes, against 46 for V1 and 4 for V2)
and then cooks the meal (30 of the 75 losses end with `cook meal with ...`).

### Suggestions for the feedback mode

The first version of this report suggested three changes (inventory in the history, no duplicate, an extra
sentence). Section 5b scored all three and none of them helps, so **I withdraw them**. With section 5c the data
says:

- The feedback history fails because it drops the recipe from the past steps. Any replacement has to keep the
  notes after each past step; the inventory or the room without the notes is useless (V7, V11, V14).
- "Feedback + notes + inventory" (V12) is the only cheaper layout worth a look: on L1 it is better than the full
  history for both models (base top-1 0.578 against 0.498; checkpoint 0.629 against 0.594) with 41 % fewer prompt
  tokens. But on L0 it is clearly worse than full (base `eat meal` 0.07 against 0.42, walkthrough probability 0.040
  against 0.236), and I have no explanation for why the room text helps on L0 and hurts on L1. I would not switch
  to it on this evidence alone.
- So for the non-reasoning policy `history_mode="full"` remains the only layout that is safe on both levels.
- All of this is teacher-forced on 40 games per level. Section 5 showed that a layout can look acceptable in mean
  rank and still win 0 / 80 (V3), so a new layout should be confirmed with an evaluation run before it is used.
  That needs a new `history_mode` in `jevtw/`, which this issue forbids; I have not written it.
- E0004 suggests reasoning closes the gap (plain-320: 0.1375 with feedback history, 0.125 with full history on
  L1), but this experiment did not test reasoning.
- Not tested: a shorter form of the notes (for example only the "Directions" lines), and the notes shown once per
