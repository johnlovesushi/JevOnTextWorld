## Summary

- **The new games have no traps, and the base model does worse on them than on the old levels**: without reasoning
  and with `full` history 2 / 80 on C0 (L0: 23 / 80) and 1 / 80 on C1 (L1: 5 / 80).
- Almost every lost episode ends on a `cook` command: either the wrong appliance at the first cooking, or a second
  cooking of an ingredient that was already cooked correctly (it burns). Wrong cut verbs are rare (0–4 episodes
  per group).
- `feedback` history wins 0 / 80 on both levels; the mechanism is the one of E0006 (the model cooks again after the
  cooking is done), now without any trap to blame.
- Continuation reasoning (320 tokens) gives the highest win rate on both levels (8 / 80), +0.075 and +0.0875 over
  no reasoning. Neither difference passes the fixed threshold of 0.1.
- By the fixed rules: Q1 "`feedback` 格式本身有问题" on C0 and on C1; Q2 not established; Q3 no level qualifies
  (0.025 and 0.0125, both below 0.1). Details and caveats in the Executor notes.

- Check 1 (rule used): every direction of the recipe other than `prepare meal` must have its command in the
  walkthrough: fry X -> `cook X with stove`, roast X -> `cook X with oven`, grill X -> `cook X with BBQ`,
  chop / slice / dice X -> `chop / slice / dice X with knife`. The directions are read from the recipe text of the
  game and compared with `metadata.ingredients` of the game's `.json`. No game fails.
- Check 2: the walkthrough is played in the game and must end in a win. No game fails.
- The walkthrough length counts the opening `inventory`, which the agent is never offered ("agent steps" is without it).
  A typical walkthrough: `examine cookbook`, `take yellow potato from counter`, `cook yellow potato with oven`,
  `take knife from counter`, `dice yellow potato with knife`, `drop knife`, `prepare meal`, `eat meal`.
- Every game has one cooking and one cutting direction. No game uses grill, none needs another room.
- **Seeds.** With the default seed, `make_games.py` draws the same (seed, recipe seed) pair for game k as it did for
  L0 and L1. The games are not the same: no game file is byte-identical and no C0 walkthrough equals its L0
  counterpart, so the default seed was kept (announced on the issue before generating).
- **C0 and C1 are nearly the same games.** They share the seeds, so game k has the same ingredient and recipe in
  both. C1 adds one `open ...` step in 24 of 40 valid games (109 of 200 train, 20 of 40 test); the other games have
  identical walkthroughs.

### C.5 Limits of these numbers

- **The walkthrough is not the only winning path.** `drop knife` is not needed (21 of the 23 won episodes of part B still hold
  the knife at `prepare meal`), and cutting before cooking wins as well (16 of the 20 won episodes of c, g and h do
  that). So a rank-1 rate of 0.00 on `drop` and part of the misses on `take knife` and `cook` are not
  mistakes; q(a\*) is a lower bound on the probability of a correct command. Preferring `prepare meal` too early
  is harmless (the game refuses it). Preferring to cook again after the cooking is done loses the game.
- The same holds for all three variants, so the comparison between them is less affected than the levels.

## D. Behaviour in the sampled episodes of part B

(`scripts/analyze_trap_free.py`): what the recipe needs is read from `metadata.ingredients`; the commands of an
episode are replayed on a small state.

- `recook`: `cook X` when the recipe ingredient X was already cooked in this episode;
- `wrong_cook`: the first cooking of the recipe ingredient uses another appliance than the recipe's;
- `wrong_cut`: the recipe ingredient is cut with another verb than the recipe's;
- `recut`: the recipe ingredient is cut a second time; `cut_no_knife`: a cut command while the knife is not held;
- `other_item`: cooking or cutting an item that is not in the recipe (this does not lose the game).

**Reading**
- Removing the traps made the task harder for the base model, not easier: the old levels could be won by
  `take`, `prepare meal`, `eat meal` with the processing already done; the new ones need a cook with the right
  appliance, a knife and the right cut, and each cooking mistake ends the game at once.
- The two dominant failures are (1) the appliance: the recipe says fry / roast, and the model has to map this to
  stove / oven — wrong in 22–43 of 80 episodes per group, and it loses at once; (2) cooking a second time,
  typically after `prepare meal` was refused because the cutting is still missing — 20–52 of 80 episodes.
- The base model without reasoning rarely takes the knife, so the cutting direction is the step it does not start.
- C0 and C1 are the same games up to one `open` step in 60 % of them, so they are not two difficulty levels in the
  sense L0 / L1 / L2 were meant to be. The results of the two levels are also close.
