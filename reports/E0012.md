## Summary

- **The decoupled verbal confidence (C) works in domain**: AUC 0.794 ± 0.024 against 0.768 ± 0.018 for A. Its
  step-adjusted AUC is 0.811, with a gain of 0.105 over the step count alone; A's is 0.829 with a gain of 0.118. C's
  Brier, 0.224, is below the constant baseline of 0.248 for all three seeds. C is still over-confident: offset 0.133,
  against A's 0.029.
- **The shared advantage (B) does not calibrate**: it barely discriminates (AUC 0.545, step gain 0.015) and is
  over-confident (offset 0.212). Its Brier, 0.292, is above the constant baseline of 0.249 for all three seeds.
- **Both verbal groups win less than A**: A 0.731, B 0.653 (−0.078), C 0.675 (−0.056), against the threshold of
  A − 0.05 = 0.681.
- **The verbal confidence is as over-confident out of domain as p_t**: the win rate is 0 on C2 and on C4. B's mean ĉ is
  0.747 on C2 and 0.718 on C4; A's mean p is 0.777 and 0.587. On C4, B is **more** confident than A.
- Hypotheses: **H13 holds** (C below B at batch 30 for 3 of 3 seeds). H14 holds for C and not for B. **H10, H11 and
  H12 do not hold.**
- With two GPUs a training batch costs 0.27 s per decision, against 0.64–0.69 s on one GPU in E0010d (a 2.4–2.5x
  speed-up, measured across levels). The verbal confidence adds about 5 s per batch to the rollout.

The within-game AUC is not part of the H14 wording, but both verbal groups are below A on it: A 0.627, B 0.497,
C 0.536. Inside one game, C ranks the decisions of won and lost episodes only slightly better than chance.

The issue's decision rules: H11 does not hold, so B and C are to be compared on how much win rate they lose. B loses
0.078 and C 0.056. Section 9 offers two readings, "the reward pushes the actions" (λ S in B's action advantage) and "the
shared parameters". C has no calibration reward in the action advantage, yet it still loses 0.056; most of the loss
therefore goes with the confidence head sharing the policy's parameters. The extra 0.022 that B loses is within the
seed noise (std 0.013–0.022). H12 does not hold either. Following the rule for "H10/H11 hold, H12 not", the verbal
confidence does not reproduce RLCR's out-of-domain advantage here, and A is the cheaper choice that also keeps
`p_options`.

## Executor notes

### Different from the issue

- Everything is on the single branch `exp/E0012-verbal-confidence`, instead of separate `task/T0008`, `task/T0009` and
  `exp/E0012` branches, because the user asked for one branch.
- The out-of-domain evaluations use `scripts/final_eval.py` on the **validation** splits of C2 and C4, with seeds 17 and 23
  and H = 38 / 102 (configs `E0012{a,b,c}-{C2,C4}.json`, as in E0011). No test set of another level was used.
- Group B uses `conf_coef` 1.0: with the shared advantage the confidence term gets the same advantage as the action, as
  the issue's B describes.
- Â^c of C uses π^c from sampling time (the rollout), not a re-computation at the start of the update; see the next
  section for why the two differ.
- There is no truncated importance sampling for the confidence term (`tis_cap` is unset for every group, as in E0010–E0011).
- The Monte Carlo test of S(c, z) uses a toy model with a flattened output layer (`tests/test_t0008.py::flat_model`):
  with the byte tokenizer the plain random model put nearly all mass on the shortest bin.

### Problems

- **Rollout vs training log-probs in B and C (found when writing the report, not during the runs).**
  `summarize_run.py` flags every B and C run: the largest |log-prob at sampling − log-prob recomputed before the
  update| is 2.1–3.1, and the mean is 0.04–0.09 per term. For A it is 0 (3e-7). The diagnostic
  `scripts/diag_verbal_layout.py` (job 8061115) shows that this is bf16 rounding, not a bug. At sampling, `decide` runs
  `verbal_forward` with confidence branches for all K options; training rescores with only the chosen option's branch.
  The folded sequence therefore has a different length and layout. A uses the same `decision_forward` layout in both
  places, so its numbers repeat exactly.

  | max abs difference (real model, real-text prompt, n = 1738, K = 12) | bf16 (sdpa) | bf16 (eager) | fp32 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | log q, all branches vs one | 0.11 | 0.10 | 4e-5 |
  | log q, folded vs unfolded full sequences | 0.11 | 0.10 | 4e-5 |
  | log π^c, all branches vs one | 0.80 | 0.61 | 1e-4 |
  | log π^c, folded vs unfolded | 1.37 | 1.84 | 1e-4 |

  In fp32 both layouts agree with separate full-sequence forwards to 1e-4, so the mask is correct. In bf16 any two
  layouts differ, including the unfolded one. The large values are on rare bins: the probability vectors themselves are
  close, e.g. 0.908 vs 0.907 on the top bin. The PPO ratio compares the update with log-probs rescored in the same layout,
  so the clipped objective is consistent. What differs is that the bins are drawn from, and evaluated on (ĉ in
  `final_eval`), the K-branch numbers. This touches all of B and C equally and does not explain the gap between them.
- A s17: the test and C4 evaluations hit "CUDA error: uncorrectable ECC error" on kn101. They were re-run once with
  `--exclude=kn101` and completed; all later jobs exclude kn101.
- B s17 training ran on kn117 at about half speed (5h39m). An extension of its time limit was refused; it finished
  inside 6 h.

### Interpretation

- The shared advantage (B) gives the confidence tokens the episode's advantage, and z dominates it. That says "this
  episode was good", not "this confidence was right", so the head learns a level (mean ĉ about 0.73) and almost no
  ranking (step gain 0.015). The decoupled advantage (C) gives each confidence its own S, centred on the
  expectation under π^c. That is a much better signal: C calibrates within 10 batches (H13), and in domain it ranks
  decisions as well as A, step effect removed.
- Neither verbal group is less over-confident out of domain. On C2 and C4 the agent never wins, but ĉ stays at 0.64–0.76,
  roughly the in-domain level. The confidence appears to follow how the game looks locally, not whether the larger
  task is within reach. A's p is lower on C4 (0.59) than on C2 (0.78), and so is C's (0.64 vs 0.76); B's barely moves.
- The training signal for the confidence costs win rate even when it does not enter the action advantage (C: −0.056).
  The two heads share every parameter, so the gradient of the confidence term changes the policy too.
- Everything here is one level, 60 batches, 3 seeds and 120 test episodes per seed. Differences of 0.02 in win rate are
  within the noise.

### Decisions needed

1. By the issue's own rule (H12 does not hold), keep A (logit + Platt) as the confidence readout? C is the better
   verbal variant if verbal confidence is pursued further.
2. Should a follow-up remove the bf16 mismatch? Two options: score the confidence after sampling with the
   single-branch layout that training uses (about one extra folded forward per decision), or run the confidence
   readout in fp32. Both change rollout numbers, so this would be a code task (T-number) and not part of E0012.
3. Is the win-rate cost of C (−0.056, borderline for H11) worth a run with a smaller `conf_coef`, or with the confidence
   loss kept away from the action tokens' parameters (e.g. a stop-gradient or LoRA split)? No such run was started.
